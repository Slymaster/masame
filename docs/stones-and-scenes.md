# Stone sets and décors: what is sourced, what is a design choice

The gallery's one-line descriptions say nothing we could not source. This page keeps the sources, so the next person who
changes a set or a décor knows what is fact and what is ours. Research done on 2026-10-20; a fact counts as sourced when
two independent sources agree. Full notes: `.claude/research/` (local, not in git).

## Stone sets

| Set | What we render | Sourced | Design choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japonaises | Nachiguro slate 22.2 mm, Hyuga clamshell 21.9 mm, biconvex, about 9 mm thick | Sizes: Nihon Kiin catalogue 2020 p. 7 (thickness grades 28 = 7.5 mm … 40 = 11.3 mm), Sensei's Library *GoStones*, ja.wikipedia 碁石 | — |
| Yunzi | Flat underneath, domed on top; matte; the black lights up with a green to blue-green rim against the light; the white soft, eggshell, slightly translucent; a louder snap | Shape, look, matte finish, rim light, Yunnan: Sensei's *Yunzi*, en/zh.wikipedia *Yunzi* / 云子, Yellow Mountain Imports go stones guide | The recipe is disputed (agate-based, sintered jade and quartz, or glass): we say nothing about it. Thickness 7–8 mm |
| Verre de Corée | Thick hardened glass (22 × 9.3–9.5 mm), very glossy, deep black, milky white | Korean hardened-glass stones: Yellow Mountain Imports product *Jangstone*; ja.wikipedia lists Japanese and Korean glass stones | There is **no distinct Korean stone tradition** (an 1893 Korean set at the Penn Museum is slate and shell, like Japan's): we never say there is |
| Jade | White nephrite with soft veils; dark spinach jade with fine veins | Jade as an elite material in Tang China: Xinhua (2019, museum founder), china.org.cn (Du Mu's poem) | No maker gives dimensions: ours are the Japanese ones |
| Galets | Flat, irregular natural pebbles, dark shale and pale sandstone | Natural stones before shell and slate in China, Korea (Baekje, Mireuksa: 14–24 mm, 2–6 mm thick) and Japan (Fujiwara-kyō): Iksan National Museum, ja.wikipedia 碁石, Research Square preprint (not peer reviewed) | Drawn larger and thicker than the historical ones (≈ 21 mm, 6 mm) so they read on a modern board |
| Agate et onyx | Banded black onyx, milky chalcedony with blue bands | Agate stones in Liao tombs: Beijing heritage bureau page (single source) | Look only; no history in the interface |
| Ambre et ébène | Polished ebony, translucent honey amber | **Nothing**: amber appears only in the Yongchang yunzi legend, ebony only as go bowls | A pairing of materials, no tradition claimed |
| Pierre de lune et obsidienne, Verre soufflé, Bronze et argent, Braise et glace, Nébuleuse | Imagined sets | — | Entirely ours; four of them are earned (see below) |
| Pierres de famille | The Japanese set ageing with your games: the shell yellows, the slate polishes | — | Derived from the number of games in Mes parties, capped at 300 |

Earned sets (visible in the gallery with their condition, nothing hidden): Bronze et argent — finish 10 games; Pierre de
lune et obsidienne — a 7-day streak; Nébuleuse — review a whole game; Braise et glace — solve a tsumego from your games.
